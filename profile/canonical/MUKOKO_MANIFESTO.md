## Ndiri nekuti tiri.

*I am because we are.*

This is not a tagline. It is not a product position. It is not something that made it into the deck because it sounds good in a boardroom.

**It is the operating system.**

Every decision we make, every feature we build, every line of code we write runs through a single filter: does this strengthen the community, or does it extract from it? That question — that ancient African question — is how we build, and how we will be held accountable for what we build.

We are Mukoko. The name is Shona for beehive. Not by accident. The hive is the most precise metaphor for what we are building: a structured, warm, self-organising community where every member contributes, every member benefits, and the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. The bee (*nyuchi*) cannot exist meaningfully without the hive (*mukoko*). The hive cannot live without the bees. This is Ubuntu in nature. This is what we are building in code.

A hive is also built one cell at a time. You can see the finished comb in your mind long before the bees have made it. This manifesto describes both: the cells already filled with honey, and the shape of the comb we are building toward. We will always tell you which is which.

This manifesto has twelve sections. Twelve, because twelve is the number of a federation. The first six build toward the centre. The last six flow from it. At the centre — sections six and seven — sit Ubuntu and its Covenants.

## § 01 — The Wound

### The platforms that exist were not built for us.

You have dozens of passwords. You have agreed to hundreds of terms of service you never read. Your digital identity is scattered across platforms built in Silicon Valley, designed for Western markets, optimised for Western profit — with Africa treated as a growth opportunity, a population to be converted into data points.

But the deeper wound is not the passwords. The deeper wound is this:

> *Platforms know you better than you know yourself — and they use that knowledge against you.*

They know your friendships, your questions, what captivates you, your most intimate conversations. They use that knowledge to capture your attention, harvest your behaviour, and sell it to advertisers. The algorithms that claim to "personalise" your experience optimise for engagement — their profit — rather than enrichment — your growth.

**You do not own your digital identity. They do.** This is not an accident. It is the business model.

For African users, it is worse. We are asked to trust platforms that do not see us, do not understand our context, do not speak our languages, and will never be accountable to our communities. When their policies harm African communities, there is no appeal that reaches anyone who cares.

A large share of Africans are unbanked or underbanked. Every platform that requires a credit card or a bank account structurally excludes much of the continent. Africa has 54 countries and more than 40 currencies, and there is no pan-African digital economic layer that does not route through Western institutions and Western currency pegs.

This is not a complaint. It is a specification. Everything Mukoko is built to solve begins here.

**Mukoko rejects this model entirely.**

## § 02 — The Answer

### Not an app. A sovereign ecosystem.

Mukoko is not "Africa's answer to WeChat" — though others will describe it that way, and they will be missing the point.

**Mukoko is Africa's super app.** A digital ecosystem built on Ubuntu philosophy, rooted in Shona culture, and designed to give every African user sovereignty over their identity, their data, their intelligence, and their digital future.

Mukoko is seventeen mini-apps — each a standalone experience — and each operates in three modes at once. In **Musha** (home), it is the room you live in. In **Basa** (work), it is the foundation the rest of the building stands on. In **Nhaka** (heritage), it is the gift you leave for those who come after — anonymised data flowing into the continental knowledge commons.

This tri-mode rule is recursive. It governs every app, every piece of infrastructure, and every data flow. If something cannot answer three questions — what does it do for the person, what does it give other services, what does it contribute to the commons — it does not belong in the architecture.

**Here is what you can use today.**

**Mukoko Account** — one login for the whole ecosystem. Sign in once; everything follows.
**Mukoko Events** — discover, host, and grow community gatherings across African cities, with RSVPs that never oversell a room, QR check-in, and circles that keep people connected between events.
**Mukoko News** — hundreds of African newsrooms in one place, with every one of the 54 African Union states in scope and live coverage measured, not claimed.
**Mukoko Weather** — weather that answers the question most weather apps skip: given this weather, can I do the thing I need to do today? Planting, spraying, open-pit work, a long drive, a match.
**Mukoko Kweli** — the Africa Trust Platform. One canonical, verified page for every place in the graph, from a national park to a street vendor, with a trust score that travels across the ecosystem.

The rest of the seventeen are designed and coming. The difference between a super app and a collection of apps is the difference between a city and a shopping mall. A mall is shops sharing a building. A city is an organism where every part interacts with every other part. We are building a city — and we are building it district by district, with people already living in the first ones.

## § 03 — The Fire

### Campfire. Where community gathers.

Most super apps are content feeds that bolted on messaging. Mukoko's design inverts this. Messaging is infrastructure, and everything else flows into and out of it.

Campfire is the architectural anchor of the design: private chats, group threads, community channels, and the place where every cross-platform event arrives. A booking confirmed in BushTrade, a reminder from Mukoko Events, a payment request from Wallet, a reply from Shamwari — in the finished hive, they all arrive in Campfire.

**Campfire is not built yet.** We say that plainly because a fire you describe but have not lit keeps no one warm.

So the fire is lit first where people already gather. Mukoko Events grew out of the Shona *nhimbe* — the communal work gathering where neighbours come together to get something done and share in the harvest. Together we gather, together we grow. Mukoko Events is live, and inside it, Circles already keep people connected between gatherings. When Campfire comes, it will carry the conversations Mukoko Events has started.

The communal fire is where African communities have always gathered. Stories were told there. Decisions were made there. Ubuntu was *lived* there.

Seventeen layers, each a mini-app, each a service, each a contribution:

## § 04 — Your Twin

### The intelligence we are building toward.

At the end of the road is your **Digital Twin**. Not a feature. Not a chatbot. Not a profile page. The destination — the thing that will make Mukoko unlike any platform ever built.

Most platforms build a model of you on *their* servers, train *their* AI on *your* data, and use *your* behaviour to serve *their* advertisers. You never see that model, never own it, and never benefit from it.

> *Your Digital Twin will invert this entirely. It will live in your sovereign pod on the Nyuchi Honeycomb — cryptographically bound to your identity, held on community-operated nodes, accessible only with your keys. No device will own it. No corporation will reach it. Mukoko will not be able to see it.*

Your Twin will unify three things other platforms keep apart: your data, your AI, and your identity. Your data feeds your AI. Your AI shapes your identity. Your identity determines what you pay attention to, which creates new data. One loop. One living digital self.

It will learn across all seventeen mini-apps, on your device, at native speed. Your personal data will be a graph — your relationships, interests, activities, and context as connected nodes — so that Shamwari reasons over connections, not keywords. It will also learn from the community through the open data commons. *I am because we are.* My Twin is mine, enriched by us.

When you talk to your Twin, you will be talking to **Shamwari** — *friend* in Shona. A friend serves; a friend does not control. The name is the constraint.

**The Twin is not started, and we will not pretend otherwise.** It needs four things first, and each is being laid now:

| Prerequisite | Where it stands |
|---|---|
| One identity across the hive | Live — Mukoko Account |
| A graph-native database that runs on your device and syncs | Being built — SiafuDB, a Bundu Foundation project |
| Your sovereign pod | Designed — the Honeycomb |
| Intelligence that runs on your phone, not ours | Designed |

Today, Shamwari already helps you find the right event, write a great description, and understand what the weather means for your day. Those features run on platform infrastructure, and your data in them is held by the platform under policy. When the pod exists, verified users' personal data moves into it — and the privacy promise stops being a policy and becomes an architectural fact.

**Privacy is not a feature we charge for. It is the foundation we are building toward, and the standard we hold ourselves to on the way.**

## § 05 — The Four Pillars

### The constraints that create freedom.

Every architectural decision flows from four pillars. They are constraints before they are principles — they rule out choices before cost or convenience gets a vote.

**Local-First.** The device is where truth lives first, and the cloud is a cache. Mukoko must work in a village on 2G and a city with no wifi. Today our apps survive weak connections through fallback chains — Weather resolves through a cache, two forecast providers, and finally seasonal estimates that always succeed, so a page never renders empty. Tomorrow, SiafuDB — an embedded graph database written in Rust and released under Apache 2.0 — will put your data on your device and sync it when connectivity returns. Infrastructure must match the reality of African lives, not assume the reality of San Francisco.

**Mobile-First.** Small screens, limited battery, constrained data, budget hardware. Our live apps are installable, fast, and built to the strictest accessibility standard we know (AAA contrast, 48-pixel touch targets). Underneath every surface we are building one core in Rust — the database, the sync, the safety gates, the cryptography — written once and carried everywhere. On top of it, each surface speaks its own language: Astro on the web, Swift on iOS, Kotlin on Android, ArkTS on HarmonyOS. Native interfaces, so that no single company controls the runtime of Africa's digital future; one Rust core, so that the guarantees are identical on every one of them. Until the native apps arrive, we ship to the web, where every phone already is.

**Open Source & Sovereign.** We build on ground that no corporation can pull from under us — and where we cannot yet, we say so and name the way out. Our frontier infrastructure is open: SiafuDB, SiafuDB-Kuzu and NTL under Apache 2.0; Mukoko Events and Mukoko Weather under MIT; Zimbabwe's travel guide under Creative Commons. **Not everything we run is open source, and that is a decision, not an accident.** Our data runs on MongoDB Atlas and Supabase. Identity runs on WorkOS. Our apps are hosted on Vercel and Cloudflare. Shamwari currently thinks with a model we did not train. Each of these sits in a published sovereignty register with a risk tier and an exit path. Flutter, Couchbase, and Databricks remain prohibited.

**Open Data.** Platform-level, anonymised, aggregate data is not Mukoko's moat. It is Mukoko's gift to Africa. Researchers, journalists, governments, NGOs, and developers should be able to ask what the community collectively knows. The first gift is already flowing: weather observations from community stations are validated and published onward to the open forecasting commons, and the Zimbabwe travel guide is free for anyone to reuse. The commons we are building — Apache Flink stripping every trace of personal data, Apache Doris holding what remains — will make the interface to Africa's knowledge a conversation, not a query language.

## § 06 — Ubuntu

### Ubuntu is not the marketing. Ubuntu is the architecture.

The Ubuntu Layer is not a moderation policy that will be quietly updated when growth demands it. It is philosophy translated into decision criteria. Every product decision passes through five questions.

**01 — Does this strengthen community?**
Not just engagement — community. Engagement can be manufactured through outrage. Community cannot.

**02 — Does this respect human dignity?**
Not just compliance — dignity. Compliance can be gamed. Dignity cannot be faked.

**03 — Does this serve the collective good?**
Not just revenue — good. Revenue is necessary; it is not sufficient justification.

**04 — Would we explain this proudly to our elders?**
Not just legally — proudly. Legal is a floor, not a ceiling.

**05 — Does this align with "I am because we are"?**
Not just "I win" — we are. If the platform wins at the community's expense, the platform has failed.

If the answer to any of these is no, we do not ship.

The questions are the gate. Behind them stands a doctrine the Bundu Foundation keeps as canonical data, open to anyone who asks.

**The Five Pillars of Ubuntu** are the spheres a person exists within: **Family** (*Mhuri*), **Community** (*Nharaunda*), **Society** (*Vanhu*), **Environment** (*Zvakatipoteredza*), and **Spirituality** (*Unhu*). Ubuntu begins in the household, widens to neighbours, extends to every stranger, is held by the natural world, and is given meaning by the sacred. Each sphere shapes a part of the platform — from account switching on a family's shared phone, to offline-first design that respects the environment and the budget handset, to memorial states for those who have passed.

**The Five Principles of Ubuntu** are the values a person practises within those spheres: **Survival** (*Kurarama*), **Solidarity** (*Kubatana*), **Compassion** (*Tsitsi*), **Respect** (*Ruremekedzo*), and **Dignity** (*Chiremerera*). Survival is why our apps work on 2G. Solidarity is Mukoko Events and Circles. Compassion is a welcome that does not punish people for the device they own. Respect is a second language that is never second-class. Dignity is no dark patterns, ever.

The Ubuntu Layer will also be a living system. Seven contribution types — content creation, review, verification, moderation, translation, curation, mentorship — are the seven ways a person strengthens the community. Contributions build reputation through graph analysis of how they ripple outward, not just how many there are. Reputation earns voice in governance, and governance flows to sustained commitment rather than to money.

Philosophy compiled into code. The doctrine is live. The scoring system is designed and coming.

## § 07 — The Seven Covenants

### What we will never do.

Manifestos are easy to write. The harder thing is specificity — and the hardest thing is honesty about which promises are already proven and which are still being built. Seven covenants. Seven oaths sealed. The oaths have not changed by a word. Where the proof is still under construction, we tell you.

---

**Covenant One: We will never sell your personal data.**

*The Sign:* Today, we do not sell personal data, and no part of our business model depends on it — we earn from fees, subscriptions, and services, not attention. Today, this is a promise kept by policy. Our destination is a promise kept by architecture: when the Honeycomb pod exists, verified users' personal data will live under their own keys, where we cannot reach it, and we cannot sell what we cannot access.

*The Consequence:* Our live products are increasingly open source, so anyone can audit how they handle data. The four-category data ownership model — personal, personal-sovereign, community, platform-open — is published, and the open commons is protected at the database schema level against personal data. If this covenant is ever broken, the community will see it in the code before we could hide it.

---

**Covenant Two: We will never design for addiction.**

*The Sign:* Attention is not our business model. We do not use variable reward loops engineered to exploit human psychology. If you open Mukoko, find what you came for, and leave — that is a success. Mukoko Weather's whole design is to answer your question and let you get on with your day.

*The Consequence:* Your Honey optimises for enrichment, not engagement. As it moves onto your device, its success is measured by whether you were served, not by minutes per session.

---

**Covenant Three: We will never let advertisers control what you see.**

*The Sign:* Advertising is permitted where transparent and relevant. It will never determine content ranking, suppress authentic voices, or invisibly shape what surfaces in your feed.

*The Consequence:* Ranking rules are ours to publish and the community's to hold us to. When the Honey algorithm runs inside your pod, advertisers will not be able to reach it at all.

---

**Covenant Four: We will never abandon African creators.**

*The Sign:* The creator economy is designed so that creators keep 80% or more of what they earn, co-authors split revenue automatically, and video creators own their content. Event hosts on Mukoko Events keep their audiences and their communities.

*The Consequence:* Creator economics will be embedded in code, not policy, so that the split cannot be quietly changed. The community can vote to adjust it through governance — but the commitment to a majority creator share is structural.

---

**Covenant Five: We will never choose proprietary technology when open source is adequate.**

*The Sign:* The frontier infrastructure we write is open: SiafuDB, SiafuDB-Kuzu, and NTL under Apache 2.0; Mukoko Events, Mukoko Weather, and our tools under MIT; the Zimbabwe travel guide under Creative Commons. Public by default, private only when necessary.

*The word that matters is* adequate. Where we run proprietary or source-available technology — MongoDB Atlas, WorkOS, Vercel, Cloudflare, the model behind Shamwari — it is because no open alternative was adequate for us yet, at our size and with our team. Every one is recorded in a published sovereignty register with a risk tier and an exit path. Some of our code is private, because it holds security, customer data handling, or commercial advantage. Most is not.

*The Consequence:* The register is public. Every repository declares its licence. If we introduce a proprietary dependency without a documented exit path, the community will see it in the register before we could hide it — and an exit path that is never exercised is not an exit path. We are working toward running every critical layer on ground no one can take from us.

---

**Covenant Six: We will never treat African languages as an afterthought.**

*The Sign:* Our live apps ship in English and Shona, and Mukoko Kweli adds Ndebele, with language independent of country — you can browse Zambia in Shona. Mukoko Lingo is a dedicated product for learning African languages, not a settings toggle.

*The Consequence:* No language is second-class. Multi-language parity is a design rule enforced in the design system, not a backlog item.

---

**Covenant Seven: We will never abandon our values when growth demands it.**

*The Sign:* This manifesto is a public contract, versioned and permanent. This version exists because we chose to tell the truth about what is built and what is not, rather than let a beautiful document drift away from the code. Fast growth that requires betraying our users is not growth. It is collapse in slow motion.

*The Consequence:* Governance will give the community the power to hold us accountable — DAO proposals against any decision that violates these covenants, with the loudest voice belonging to those with the longest record of contribution. Until that governance is live, the accountability is this document, the open code, the published register, and the Bundu Foundation's place at the top of the stack.

---

In short:

We will not manipulate your attention. We will not harvest your private data. We will not sell your behaviour to advertisers. We will not charge for privacy. We will not lock you in. We will not abandon African users for "global markets." We will not build dark patterns. We will not pretend algorithms are neutral while encoding exploitation. **And we will not pretend something is built when it is not.**

## § 08 — Ubuntu Economics

### Value provided, not data harvested.

We reject surveillance capitalism. We reject the attention economy. We reject data brokerage. Mukoko earns through value provided.

**Transaction fees** — small, transparent fees on commerce and ticketing.
**Premium features** — enhanced tools for organisers, creators, and power users.
**Creator economy** — a small share when creators monetise; they keep 80% or more.
**Enterprise partnerships** — Nyuchi products and API access for African businesses.
**Token economy** — minimal, transparent fees on token exchanges.

**The first fire to earn its keep is the gathering.** Mukoko Events is where our revenue begins: tools for organisers, fair per-ticket fees, and payments that work for the informal and community events the incumbents ignore — the church fundraiser, the township football tournament, the nhimbe itself. We dropped NFT ticketing; it solved a problem our communities do not have.

The MUKOKO economic system is designed as four tokens, all issued and governed by the **Bundu Foundation**.

The **MIT** (MUKOKO Identity Token) is soulbound — permanently yours, non-transferable, anchored to your date of birth, participating in three temporal pools whose value rises as the community ages together. It is not a currency. It is your temporal identity in the ecosystem. When you pass, your MIT becomes ancestral: your age fixes, your governance weight rests, and the patterns you chose to preserve join the continental heritage commons.

The **MXT** (MUKOKO Exchange Token) is transferable — for payments, commerce, tickets, creator rewards, and enterprise fees. Elastic supply, governed by the EmissionController: 3 billion at genesis, 10,000 MXT for each new verified person, an annual ceiling of 15%, and 30% of platform fees burned. **There is no hard cap.** Growth benefits every existing member. This is Ubuntu expressed as token economics.

The **NST** (Nyuchi Storage Token) governs storage and node incentives on the Nyuchi Honeycomb — the network that will hold your sovereign pod — so the Honeycomb can grow without depending on any one company's capital. The **NHC** (Nyuchi Honeycomb Coin) powers network operations, with static gas prices for MIT holders so the cost of digital sovereignty does not swing with speculation.

**The tokens are designed, not issued, and we have not yet reached the stage of putting them on a blockchain.** We will not set a date for that before we are ready. Before any token exists, the Bundu Foundation must be incorporated and the legal character of each token settled — responsibly, and where no law yet exists, by helping to write one. We would rather be right than first.

No bank account will be required. Value will move over the mobile money people already use, across 54 countries and more than 40 currencies, without routing through Western institutions or Western currency pegs. Holding people's money is a trust we take seriously: stored value will be safeguarded from day one, under the proper licences.

## § 09 — Sovereignty

### Africa's digital future must be Africa's to decide.

Sovereignty is not a slogan we have already earned. It is a measure we hold ourselves to, layer by layer, and publish.

**We know where our truth lives.** Three sources of truth: two platform, one personal. The platform's relational truth lives in PostgreSQL. Its operational content lives in MongoDB. The personal truth — your pod on the Honeycomb — is the one we are still building. Everything else in the architecture is a pipe, a cache, or the commons, and can be rebuilt from those three.

**We know what we rent.** Today, some of our truth runs on managed services. MongoDB Atlas holds our content; its licence is not open source, but its source is available and its community server runs anywhere, so we classify it as a medium risk with a way out. WorkOS holds sign-in, with our own records of every person and organisation mirrored in our database. Vercel and Cloudflare serve our apps, but nothing at the edge is a source of truth. Shamwari thinks today with a model we did not build, behind a gateway that lets us change it. Every one of these is in the register, with a tier and an exit path.

*Speed is rented. Truth is owned.* In v4 we wrote that as a fact. In v5 we write it as the standard we measure ourselves against — and today, on that standard, some of our truth is still rented. We say so because a sovereignty claim you cannot audit is just marketing.

**We are building the ground we will own.** The frontier infrastructure is ours and it is open:

**SiafuDB** — named for the African army ant, small and embedded, but the ecosystem collapses without it — is a graph database built to run on your phone, at the edge, and inside your pod, syncing graphs between them. It will replace sync protocols that could not carry the shape of a person's life.

**NTL** — the Neural Transfer Layer, an open protocol — replaces the request-and-response pattern of the old web with signals that strengthen with use, designed with a post-quantum path from the first line.

**Mzizi** — *root* — is the open architecture and design system every Mukoko and Nyuchi surface is built on, with seven minerals of African earth as its colours, accessibility guarantees that cannot be switched off, and a self-healing agent, Fundi, that turns failures into fixes.

The analytical commons — Flink and Doris, the open engines that will hold Africa's anonymised knowledge — is designed and waiting for the scale that justifies it. When it comes, the interface to Africa's knowledge will be a conversation, not a query language: an analytical agent that answers natural-language questions with quality-annotated results and documented methods.

We are building from Zimbabwe — a country that has survived economic collapse, international isolation, and institutional failure, and whose people have, despite all of it, continued to build, teach, trade, and create. That resilience is not a footnote. **It is the architecture.**

## § 10 — The Hive

### The hive is bigger than the app.

Mukoko does not stand alone. It is one of three pillars of an ecosystem.

**The Bundu Foundation** stewards the commons. It owns the standards, the doctrine, and the open projects — Mzizi, SiafuDB, NTL, the Zimbabwe Information Platform, Harare Metro, Bundu Education — and it will be the sole issuer of all four tokens. It sits at the top of the stack, so that the community's voice is embedded in the ecosystem's governance rather than bolted on afterwards. Its incorporation is our next legal step.

**Nyuchi Africa** operates everything that runs — identity, the API gateway, the databases, the pipelines, the edge — and builds the enterprise products: an open and growing set of doors into the ecosystem. Today they include the Nyuchi API Platform, Web Services, Learning, Medical, Logistics, Tools, Pay, Masasa, and StationKit, the solar-powered weather and soil station built for African smallholder farms. A doctor on Nyuchi Medical and a patient on Mukoko Health will be in the same world, connected through the same Mukoko Account.

**Mukoko** is the surface people live in — seventeen mini-apps, all drawing from the same geographic knowledge graph that Mukoko Kweli verifies. Kweli has grown out of Barstool: what began as reviews of bars and restaurants is now the trust page for every place in Africa, from a national park to a street vendor. A place verified once in Kweli carries its trust everywhere. An event on Mukoko Events shows weather from Mukoko Weather. A station in a farmer's field improves the forecast for the whole district.

Every surface in the hive shares the same forty interest categories, the same identity, the same design system, the same Ubuntu doctrine, and the same commitment to African sovereignty.

Nyuchi Africa is governed by a three-way voting lock — the founder, the family, and the Bundu Foundation. Investors are welcome to invest in the idea and in the community; they hold economic shares, not the steering wheel. That structure is being formalised now.

## § 11 — The Vision

### The long game.

We are building from Zimbabwe in 2026, with all 54 countries as the baseline, not the aspiration. The constraints are the philosophy. We build local-first because we live local. We build for weak connections because our users have them. We build on open source because we cannot afford dependencies we do not control — and we are honest about the ones we still have.

The vision is not a timeline. It is a covenant escalation — each stage broader than the last, each earning the right to the next.

**Stage 1 — The Person.** Individual sovereignty. It begins with one identity across the whole hive — live today as Mukoko Account — and it is fulfilled when that identity becomes your Digital Twin, living in your own pod, reasoning over your own graph, on your own device.

**Stage 2 — The Conversation.** Campfire. Relational sovereignty. People communicating on infrastructure they trust.

**Stage 3 — The Circle.** Circles. Community sovereignty. The persistent social fabric — already forming around Mukoko Events' gatherings.

**Stage 4 — The Gathering.** Mukoko Events. Physical convergence. Where the digital platform produces physical community — events, festivals, meetups, ceremonies, the nhimbe itself. This is where the fire is lit first.

**Stage 5 — The Market.** BushTrade and Wallet. Economic sovereignty. Commerce and exchange without routing through Western institutions, on mobile money, across 54 countries.

**Stage 6 — The Nation.** Zimbabwe first, then the region, then the continent. Infrastructure that African governments and institutions can trust because it is built by Africans and accountable to African communities.

**Stage 7 — The Legacy.** Open source, open data, open protocols. The civilisational gift. A generation that cannot imagine platforms that exploit their data, because they grew up in a hive where the honey was theirs. The tools through which Africa's knowledge is explored are open and named. Data quality is monitored, not assumed. Methods are documented, not hidden.
